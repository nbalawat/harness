This memorandum records the risk assessment of Meridian Halcyon Holdings Limited for the purpose of corporate onboarding. 2. Beneficial ownership

Two natural persons hold at or above the 25 per cent threshold: Anneke Voss at 41.0 per cent and Rustam Delev at 27.5 per cent. Both are declared on the beneficial ownership declaration received 21 July 2026 and signed by each. The remaining 31.5 per cent is distributed across four parties, none reaching the threshold and none therefore requiring declaration under the checklist.[2]

The declared chain runs through three layers of holding companies, two of them in Cyprus and one in the Seychelles. A structure chart dated 20 July 2026 was supplied and is consistent with the declaration. One of the three directors is a nominee appointed by a Limassol corporate services provider; nominee arrangements are treated as a structural complexity indicator under the risk rating matrix rather than as an adverse finding in themselves.[3]

Evidence not present in the grounding set: no independent verification of the source of wealth of Rustam Delev appears in the case record, and the policy corpus does not contain a source-of-wealth standard for holdings between 25 and 30 per cent. The agent makes no finding on this point. A human reviewer must determine whether further evidence is required before adoption.

3. Jurisdiction exposure

The applicant is incorporated in the Republic of Cyprus, an EU member state, and operates a registered branch in the Seychelles. The matrix classifies the Seychelles as an elevated-exposure jurisdiction on account of corporate secrecy provisions and limited registry transparency; the branch is operational rather than nominal, with declared settlement activity attributed to it.[4] Expected settlement spans six jurisdictions, of which one further — the United Arab Emirates — carries an elevated classification.

4. Sanctions screening

Screening executed 22 July 2026 09:02 UTC against the consolidated list set returned one name match, on the director surname Delev, adjudicated a false positive by the screening workflow on date-of-birth and nationality mismatch, with the adjudication recorded in the trail.[5] No true positive was returned. The sanctions band override was accordingly not triggered; had a true positive been confirmed, the case would sit in the High band irrespective of the weighted computation.

The workflow computed a weighted score of 77.95, rounded to 78, under Risk Rating Matrix v2.1, placing the applicant in the High band (70–100). The computation is mechanical; neither the agent nor any human role can alter the figure, and the derivation is set out in full at Section IV.[6] The High band carries a six-month review cadence and reserves the approval decision to a Compliance Officer.

6. Matters for the human reviewer

The agent identifies three points on which the corpus supports no conclusion: the source of wealth of the 27.5 per cent holder; the commercial rationale for the Seychelles branch, which is asserted in the submission but not evidenced; and whether the nominee directorship warrants a request for the underlying nominee agreement. Automated approval is prohibited

No scheduled job, no workflow step, no integration and no agent may record an approval, a rejection or any other status change. The approval endpoint accepts only a request bearing the session of a named human holding the required tier for the band; every other caller is refused and the refusal is written to the trail. There is no service account with approval capability, and none can be created.

Authorisation is enforced server-side

Disabled buttons and hidden sections are conveniences for the reader. Every action is re-authorised on the server against the caller's identity, role and the band of the case at the moment of the request. A forged or replayed request from a browser is rejected regardless of what the interface displayed, and the attempt is recorded with the caller's identity.

Standing rules

  • Least privilege. A role holds the narrowest capability set that lets it do its work, and nothing beyond it. Capability is granted by role, never by exception to an individual.
  • Attribution. Every write carries a named individual and the role held at that moment. The trail records the role as at the act, so a later promotion never rewrites history.
  • Auditor isolation. The auditor role holds no write capability on any path — not on cases, memoranda, scores, notifications or the trail itself. Read and export only.
  • Segregation of duty. The role that drafts or requests a memorandum does not thereby gain authority to approve the case it concerns; band authority is decided by the matrix, not by involvement.
  • No waiver below the officer tier. The waive capability does not exist for an analyst of any grade, in the interface or on the server.

Roles in force

KYC Analyst · Senior Analyst · Compliance Officer · Auditor. Four, and no fifth. New capability is added to an existing role by policy amendment, not by creating a bespoke role for a person.

The auditor's position

Read everything, write nothing. Full sight of every case, every memorandum version, every score derivation and every trail entry, with unrestricted export. No approval, no assignment, no annotation.

On hiding controls

The interface hides what a role may not do so that the desk reads honestly. Security, however, rests entirely on the server-side check; the interface is not a control.
